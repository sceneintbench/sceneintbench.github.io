SceneIntBench: Benchmarking Interaction-Level Physical Plausibility in 3D Indoor Scene Generation

Wendi Ying1 Daochang Liu1 Leonid Sigal2,3 Ajmal Saeed Mian1
1The University of Western Australia 2University of British Columbia 3Canada CIFAR AI Chair

Abstract

Existing evaluations of text-conditioned 3D indoor scene generation largely emphasize semantic fidelity and physical validity at rest, leaving interaction-time physical reliability insufficiently assessed. To address this gap, we introduce SceneIntBench, a unified benchmark centered on interaction readiness: it executes prescribed manipulation actions (e.g., pushing, opening, and surface probing) in physics simulation and measures task success, capability and progress, post-interaction integrity, and dynamic plausibility. Multi-view semantic and static physical evaluations complement these metrics and help explain interaction outcomes. We systematically evaluate 12 state-of-the-art scene generation systems across 10 room types. Our results reveal that strong semantic and static physical performance does not necessarily translate into interaction readiness: a system with near-perfect static plausibility completes only 11.54% of interactions, 7 of the 12 systems produce scenes that cannot support any interaction, and even the best system succeeds in only 36.92% of prescribed tasks. These findings highlight two fundamental challenges in generating interaction-ready scenes: providing the necessary physical interaction capabilities and ensuring reliable physical behavior under active manipulation.
